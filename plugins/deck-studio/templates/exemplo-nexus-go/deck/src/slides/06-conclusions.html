Answers to the research questions (1/4)

RQ1How do different representations of the STRING PPI network data impact the quality of functional prediction across the three GO domains?The representation and its inductive bias matter. The unreduced score profile leads the pooled alternatives by up to 4.53 pp on the three-ontology mean, and architectures that impose a 1-D order or 2-D locality on coordinates that are training proteins in arbitrary order perform worse in all three domains.
RQ2To what extent does fixed propagation of ProtT5 embeddings over the STRING network improve upon the network-only representation, and how does this effect vary across BP, MF, and CC?Large, but mostly from the sequence embedding. On test, the network-only route trails the graph-free ProtT5 control by 16.44 (BP), 16.39 (CC) and 23.95 pp (MF); propagation then adds +1.87 BP and +2.15 CC over that control, and nothing in MF, where the optimal depth is zero. The graph corrects the representation; it does not replace it.

Answers to the research questions (2/4)

RQ3How do different strategies, ranging from simple graph embeddings to fixed non-parametric propagation and GNN approaches, compare under a common experimental protocol and across vector- and image-based classification architectures?By mean validation wFmax: Late 67.06 > Early 65.84 > Fixed 65.12 > GNN 63.32. The graph-embedding methods of the literature fall below the term-frequency prior in at least one domain, and among the routes run with both heads the ordering holds for each of them.
RQ4Do early- and late-fusion ensembles improve the primary evaluation metric over their individual fixed-propagation and GNN components?Both help, and combining predictions helps more than combining representations. Early gains 0.72 pp on the mean over the better representation it fuses. Late gains +2.18 BP, +1.85 CC and +1.26 MF over its better component; one scalar beats five stacking meta-classifiers, at the cost of one parameter fitted on validation.

Answers to the research questions (3/4)

RQ5How do the propagation depth and the retention coefficient affect predictive performance, and at what point does additional propagation begin to dilute the protein-specific signal?With the vector head the optimum is one or two steps, and dilution starts right after: performance falls 13.60 (BP), 12.80 (CC) and 17.60 pp (MF) from the peak to ten steps. In BP and CC retention dominates, with \alpha between 0.7 and 0.8; in MF the optimal depth is zero. A protein with no neighbours is only scaled by \alpha^{T}.
RQ6How does the proposed approach compare against established state-of-the-art network-driven algorithms when all methods are evaluated using the same data splits and transductive graph setting?NEXUS-GO/Late has the best value in every column of the three test tables, with one exception: AuPRC in MF, where NEXUS-GO/Early leads. Its margin over the strongest baseline is +9.77 BP, +8.67 CC and +4.93 MF pp of wFmax, and Smin agrees.

Answers to the research questions (4/4)

RQ7Can the proposed methodology generalize effectively when applied directly to global, aggregated-evidence networks, without the need to partition the graph by specific species or evidence channels?On the network tested, yes: NEXUS-GO runs on one global, multi-species network with its evidence channels already aggregated, needs no partitioning stage and reaches the highest wFmax of the comparison, while the configurations built for several separate networks, deepNF among them, do worst on it. This shows applicability, not superiority over partitioning by species or channel: that counterfactual was not run.

Limitations

  • Single seed; the 0.5 pp floor is a reading convention, not a confidence interval
  • Random protein-level split: transductive interpolation, possible homology leakage
  • STRING scores may encode annotation knowledge (circularity)
  • Fixed vs learned is not a single-factor ablation (\alpha^{T} scaling, normalization)
  • Baselines adapted to one aggregated network and a reduced label space

State these before the committee does.

Each one is documented in the dissertation with its consequence.

Next steps

Separate the graph by organism, or one model per organism
Keep STRING evidence channels apart (typed or parallel edges)
Heterogeneous graphs (proteins, terms, evidence); compare with ProtHGT
Sampled aggregation (GraphSAGE) for a cheaper learned route
Richer node features: structure, domains, expression; most promising for MF

The two most immediate are taxonomic scope and evidence channels.

MF is where the network contributed least, so new node features matter most there.
