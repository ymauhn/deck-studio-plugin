Two generations, three decisions

  • Every model: representation → propagation → prediction head
  • Generation 1: network only
    • About one third isolated: null descriptor
  • Generation 2 (NEXUS-GO): ProtT5 + network

Every model decomposes into the same three decisions: representation, propagation and prediction head.

The limitation of the first generation is a property of its input, about one third of the proteins get a null descriptor, and it motivates the second.

Generation 1: the network alone

  • Input: row of the weighted adjacency, against training proteins
  • Pooling: raw, max, avg
  • Vector route: MLP or 1-D CNN
  • Image route: outer-difference matrix |v_i - v_j|, 224 × 224, ResNet-50 or ConvNeXt-Tiny
  • Floor: a naive guilt-by-association baseline

The descriptor is already a one-hop aggregation, so there is no separate propagation stage.

The image route imports a convolutional bias into a non-visual vector, as SUPERMAGOv2 and DeepInsight did with isolated descriptors.

An isolated protein has no input

  • Null profile → null matrix → uniform image
  • The head can only return the class prior
  • No architecture can recover what is absent

Requirement: a representation that exists for every protein

For a third of the corpus the first generation sees nothing.

The amino-acid sequence satisfies the requirement by construction, and that is where the second generation starts.

Generation 2: NEXUS-GO

Sequence → ProtT5 → \mathbf{H}^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{3072}
STRING → weighted graph W → P = W D_c^{-1}
Routes: /Fixed · /GNN · /Early · /Late

Two inputs, two propagation routes and two ways of combining them.

The first generation is reported as a topology-only control and carries no route name.

ProtT5-XL-U50, three layers, frozen

  • Chunks of at most 1,022 residues; masked mean-pool, then mean over chunks
  • Layers 24, 23, 22, concatenated: 3 × 1024 = 3072 dimensions
  • Exists for every protein, isolated or not

Reading three adjacent layers avoids committing to one point between the pretraining objective and structural signal, at no tuning cost.

Extraction is inherited from SUPERMAGOv2.

From records to an operator

  • Symmetric, no self-loops, sparse COO: at most 1,133,132 stored entries (dense: 25 to 35 GB)
  • Deduplication: max keeps the score; sum multiplies it by the record count
  • Column-stochastic P = W D_c^{-1}: weights are relative to each protein's neighbours

Repeated records carry no extra score, so summing rewards how often a pair was written down. The maximum keeps the STRING score on its own scale.

Normalization does not undo the reordering.

Fixed propagation: no parameters

\mathbf{H}^{(h)} = \alpha\, \mathbf{H}^{(h-1)} + (1-\alpha)\, P^{\top} \mathbf{H}^{(h-1)}

h = 1, \dots, T

  • Closed form: hop k gets weight \binom{T}{k}\, \alpha^{T-k} (1-\alpha)^{k}
  • Computed once, cached, reused every epoch
  • T = 0 or \alpha = 1: the graph is switched off (internal control)

α retains part of the previous step, not of the original, so it slows mixing instead of re-injecting the input.

The weights over hop counts are binomial with mean T(1 − α). Unlike personalized PageRank, the initial state is never reintroduced.

Propagation on four real proteins

  • Connected proteins keep their structure and absorb their neighbourhood
  • An isolated protein becomes \alpha^{T} \mathbf{H}^{(0)}: same direction, smaller scale
  • So degree-zero gains cannot come from neighbourhood information

The rescaling is a structural property of the operator, not a tuned setting.

The learned route adds self-loops and never mixes an isolated node with others.

Learned propagation: GCN and GAT

  • GCN: symmetric normalization of W + I, then a trained transform
  • GAT: attention over neighbours, 4 heads, edge score as attribute
  • 2 layers, width 1024, output \mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024} for the head
  • Full-graph forward on every step: about 289 propagations per epoch
GCN
\mathbf{H}^{(\ell+1)} = \phi\!\left( \tilde{D}^{-1/2}\,\tilde{W}\,\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\,\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}\,\Theta^{(\ell)} \right), \quad \tilde{W} = W + I
GAT
e_{ij} = \operatorname{LeakyReLU}\!\left( \mathbf{q}^{\top} \left[\, \Theta \mathbf{h}_i \,\|\, \Theta \mathbf{h}_j \,\|\, \Theta_e\, w_{ij} \,\right] \right)
a_{ij} = \frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}(i) \cup \{i\}} \exp(e_{ik})}
fixed computed once learned recomputed every optimization step

The GCN takes the STRING confidence as an edge weight; the GAT re-estimates it.

Exact propagation with a mini-batch loss forces a full-graph pass for every batch, which is where the cost comes from.

Vector head and vision head

  • Vector: MLP [1024, 512], batch norm, dropout 0.4
  • Backbones: ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-Tiny (ImageNet), fully fine-tuned
  • Vision: \sigma(\mathrm{clip}(z)) split into three 32 × 32 channels, one per ProtT5 layer

The image is built inside the forward pass and never written to disk.

Neighbouring pixels are neighbouring embedding coordinates only by storage order, a caveat that the results confirm.

Training objective: ProteinLoss

\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{ZLPR}} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{wF}_1^{\text{GO}}\bigr) \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{wF}_1^{\text{prot}}\bigr)
  • ZLPR separates positive from negative terms at threshold zero
  • Two soft, IC-weighted F_1 factors: one across terms, one across proteins
  • Brings training closer to the evaluation criterion

Inherited from InterLabelGO+ through the group's sequence-only line.

Combining the fixed and learned routes

  • Early: join representations, train one head (concat 4096 or block-wise add 3072)
  • Late: train each route, combine probabilities
  • Eleven late rules: linear \gamma, max, min, IC sigmoid, degree sigmoid, master equation, five stacking meta-classifiers
  • Coefficients chosen on validation (71 configurations per ontology)

The two routes are alternatives, no model is shared.

Early fusion lets one head learn how to weigh the sources; late fusion leaves each route to its own optimization and needs no training to combine.
