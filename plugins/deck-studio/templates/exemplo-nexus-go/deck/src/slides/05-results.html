170 runs, one funnel

  • 170 runs, about 136.3 GPU-hours (147 on an RTX A5500)
  • Each stage picks its winner on validation and hands it on
  • Test read once, at the end

The selection never touches the test partition. Every reported test number is a single evaluation of a frozen configuration.

Two stations are not pure validation picks: max deduplication rests on an analytic argument (all 170 runs use it; the sum figures come from the earlier campaign), and ConvNeXt-Tiny is carried forward on a 0.57 pp margin plus the VISAPP campaign.

Generation 1 settles three choices

  • Naive term-frequency baseline: 43.56 mean wFmax
  • ProteinLoss beats binary cross-entropy: +5.47 pp mean (+8.87 in MF)
  • MLP on the raw profile wins: 44.51; best 1-D CNN 41.16
  • Vision backbones: 36.99 to 37.62

Topology alone barely clears a predictor that looks at nothing: 0.95 pp on the mean, and below it in BP.

The vector route wins, and ConvNeXt-Tiny is carried forward on a 0.57 pp margin plus the earlier VISAPP campaign.

One or two hops, high retention

  • Peak depth: T = 1 BP, T = 2 CC, T = 0 MF
  • Best retention: \alpha = 0.7 BP, \alpha = 0.8 CC
  • Beyond the peak, performance falls 12.80 to 17.60 pp by T = 10
  • The graph corrects the protein's own representation; it does not replace it

In MF no amount of propagation helps; the best configuration switches the graph off.

In CC everything below α = 0.4 is worse than everything above it; in BP the single exception is α = 0.9.

The vision head wants deep propagation

  • Peak depth: T = 7 BP, T = 6 CC, T = 0 MF; a plateau, not a peak
  • Collapse at \alpha \le 0.2: isolated nodes shrink as \alpha^{T} (10^{-7} at T = 7)
  • Gains over T = 0: +5.84 BP, +2.93 CC (vector head: +1.85, +0.92)
  • Its best BP value, 52.17, is still below the vector head's graph-free 53.36

The vision head extracts less from the node itself, so dilution costs it less and the neighbourhood average helps more.

MF peaks at T = 0 in both heads, the strongest form of that observation.

Learning the aggregation does not pay

  • GCN beats GAT in all 6 pairs (3.88 to 9.31 pp), and in 30 of 30 fusion pairs
  • Fixed 65.12 vs GCN 63.32 mean wFmax
  • GCN ahead in BP by 0.55 (a tie); behind in CC by 0.59 and MF by 5.35
  • Deduplication: max never loses beyond noise; GAT gains most (+3.83 vision)

Attention does not recover a better weighting than the STRING score it was handed.

A plausible reason: the loss is masked to training nodes, so attention parameters see gradient only through a two-hop receptive field.

Fusion helps; combining predictions helps more

  • Early, one configuration (add, GCN, vector): 65.80, +0.68 over fixed alone
  • Late, linear \gamma (0.50 BP, 0.55 CC and MF): 67.06, best in every ontology
  • Gains over the better component: +2.18 BP, +1.85 CC, +1.26 MF
  • Stacking importances: the two probabilities carry almost all the weight

A single scalar beats every elaborate rule, including gradient boosting. The auxiliary features do not predict which route is right, and the conditional rules converged to the flattest sigmoid in the grid.

The ensemble fits one extra parameter on validation.

Vector head wins 12 of 12

  • Margin on the mean: +6.62, +3.96, +2.69, +3.35 pp across the four stages
  • No trend toward zero
  • Neither image has the locality a convolution assumes

Generation 1 images index arbitrary training proteins; generation 2 images reshape embedding coordinates.

Test set: NEXUS-GO vs five methods

  • NEXUS-GO/Late best in every column of the three tables, except AuPRC in MF
  • wFmax margin over the strongest baseline: +9.77 BP, +8.67 CC, +4.93 MF
  • Smin: 17.12 vs 20.11 (BP), 8.22 vs 9.67 (CC), 5.14 vs 5.67 (MF)
  • The ProtT5 baseline alone already beats every literature method on wFmax

Every baseline was retrained on the same corpus and scored with the same code.

Three of them lose their multi-network mechanism on a single aggregated graph, so the ordering describes these adapted configurations, not the original papers.

Every protein gets a prediction

  • NEXUS-GO: 100% of the test partition in BP, CC, MF
  • DeepGraphGO and SEGT-GO: 95.1–96.3% (homology search)
  • Mashup, MELISSA, deepNF: 63.6–68.7% (network members only)

Missing proteins count as misses in the comparison, so part of the distance to the network-only methods is reach, not ranking quality.

The proteins they cannot reach are exactly the band where sequence-aware routes score highest.

A thirteenth of the time per epoch

  • Fixed + vector: 5.24 s/epoch, 6.4 GiB peak
  • GCN alone: 68.76 s/epoch, 14.1 GiB; GAT 161.41 s, 20.7 GiB
  • Matches or beats the GCN at about 1/13 of the time and under half the memory
  • Early fusion costs the same as fixed propagation once \mathbf{g} is stored

The fixed operator runs once per experiment; the learned ones recompute a full-graph propagation at every optimization step.

Early-fusion cost excludes training the GCN checkpoint it consumes.

The graph helps where there is graph

  • Every ProtT5 route scores worse on connected than on isolated proteins
  • The graph-free control already shows it, so the graph does not cause it
  • The graph's gain grows with degree: up to +9.05 BP, +8.71 CC, +4.64 MF at degree ≥ 50

Hubs tend to be multifunctional and harder for a sequence model. In BP the hardest proteins have degree 3 to 5, not zero.

Degree-zero differences come from rescaling and refitting, so read topological gains from degree one upward.
